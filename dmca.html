DMCA & Copyright Policy

CineVault respects intellectual property rights. This page explains our approach to copyright concerns and provides guidance for submitting content removal requests.

Last Updated: November 24, 2026

Important Information

CineVault aims to respect copyright and intellectual property rights. This policy describes a process for reporting potentially infringing material. It does not establish that CineVault qualifies for any particular statutory safe harbour or that a specific country's copyright procedure automatically applies.

1. Copyright Policy

CineVault respects the rights of copyright owners, creators, filmmakers, studios, distributors and other intellectual property holders.

We do not intend to knowingly host, publish or distribute content in violation of applicable intellectual property laws.

If you believe that material referenced or displayed on CineVault infringes your copyright, you may submit a detailed written notice for review.

2. Content and External Links

CineVault may provide movie information, descriptions, posters, images, promotional materials and links to external websites or third-party services.

Some external destinations may be operated by independent third parties. CineVault does not necessarily own, control or operate those websites.

The presence of an external link does not mean that CineVault owns, endorses or controls the content available at that destination.

Copyright complaints should identify the specific material and explain the relationship between the reported material and the copyright owner's rights.

3. How to Submit a Copyright Notice

A copyright owner or an authorised representative may prepare a written notice containing the information below.

  1. Identification of the copyrighted work: Describe the copyrighted work you believe has been infringed.
  2. Identification of the reported material: Provide the exact CineVault page URL and, where possible, the specific external URL or material concerned.
  3. Your contact information: Provide your full name, organisation (if applicable), postal address, email address and other relevant contact details.
  4. Authority or ownership: Explain whether you are the copyright owner or authorised to act on behalf of the owner.
  5. Good-faith statement: Explain why you believe the disputed use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent or applicable law.
  6. Accuracy statement: Confirm that the information in your notice is accurate and that you are authorised to submit it.
  7. Signature: Include an electronic or physical signature where required by the applicable procedure.

4. Review and Removal Process

After receiving a sufficiently detailed copyright complaint through an available communication channel, CineVault may review the submitted information.

Depending on the nature of the complaint and applicable law, we may take appropriate action, including:

  • Reviewing the identified page or material.
  • Requesting additional information or clarification.
  • Removing or disabling access to material under our control when appropriate.
  • Removing or correcting an external link where appropriate.
  • Taking other reasonable steps consistent with applicable legal obligations.

Submission of a complaint does not guarantee removal. Each request is subject to review.

5. Counter-Notice Information

If material has been removed or access has been restricted following a copyright complaint, an affected person may submit a written response explaining why the action was mistaken.

A counter-notice, where applicable under the relevant legal procedure, should include:

  • Identification of the material that was removed or restricted.
  • The original location of the material, if known.
  • Your full name and contact information.
  • An explanation of why you believe the removal or restriction was incorrect.
  • Any relevant evidence supporting your position.
  • Required declarations and signatures under the applicable law.

A counter-notice does not automatically restore removed material. Any response will be considered according to applicable law and the circumstances of the case.

6. Misrepresentation and False Claims

Copyright notices should be submitted honestly and in good faith.

Submitting knowingly false, misleading or fraudulent copyright claims may result in legal consequences under applicable law.

Before submitting a notice, ensure that you have the necessary rights or authority and that the reported material is accurately identified.

7. Repeat Infringement

Where appropriate and consistent with applicable law, CineVault may take action regarding repeated copyright complaints involving material or links under its control.

Such action may include restricting access to repeatedly reported material or taking other reasonable measures.

8. Third-Party Websites

CineVault may link to websites and services that are independently operated by third parties.

We do not control the content, policies, availability or practices of those external services.

If your complaint concerns material hosted entirely on a third-party website, you may also need to contact that website's operator or hosting provider directly.

9. Applicable Law

Copyright rights and enforcement procedures vary between countries and jurisdictions.

This policy is intended as general information about CineVault's approach to copyright concerns. It is not a legal opinion, does not replace applicable law and does not create statutory rights or obligations beyond those established by relevant law.

Nothing in this policy limits any rights or remedies available under applicable law.

10. Policy Updates

CineVault may update this policy when necessary to reflect changes in website operations, applicable requirements or internal procedures.

The latest revision date will be displayed at the top of this page.
